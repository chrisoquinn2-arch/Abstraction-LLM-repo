# Foundational Research and Governance Protocol
## Outset Item 1 — Operating Standards

**Version:** 0.1 — Draft for review  
**Status:** Proposed; **not yet adopted**  
**Authority to draft:** Ratified Charter v0.3.1, Articles V–VIII and X; Research Director instruction to work through Article VIII outset items sequentially  
**Applies when adopted:** Program-wide; subordinate protocols may specify details within delegated authority  
**Current scope:** Outset item 1 only. Does not establish resource taxonomies, import research knowledge, decide problem-worthiness, or select experiments.

---

## 1. Function and boundaries

This instrument proposes how the program authorizes research, assesses the validity and scope of claims, handles delegations, reviews changes, and maintains governing procedures.

It does **not** perform any scientific investigation or admit earlier conversations, literature summaries, or experimental ideas as findings. Those are to be handled later under the appropriate outset items.

A distinction between the *authority to propose a change* and the *authority to make it binding* applies throughout.

## 2. Source of authority and delegated action

The chain of program authority is:

1. Ratified founding charter and properly ratified amendments.
2. Approved subordinate governance and research protocols valid under that charter.
3. Valid delegations, project authorizations, and decisions made under those protocols.
4. Local agent instructions and plans within the scope of those delegations.

Each consequential action or binding change must have an identifiable authorizing source. The source may confer permission explicitly or through a reasonable, accountable interpretation of its scope. Ordinary actions within that scope do not require repeated director approval.

Neither a tool's technical ability to perform an operation nor an agent's belief that an operation is useful constitutes permission.

An agent may propose new permissions or procedures without already possessing authority to enact them.

## 3. Changes to authority propagate downstream

A change to an authorizing instrument requires examination of dependent delegations, permissions, obligations, and subordinate protocols.

- Record the changed source, effective date, and known dependent instruments.
- Determine whether each dependent instrument remains authorized, requires revision, or has lost its authority.
- Suspend or restrict any dependent action whose authorization is now materially in doubt, without unnecessarily freezing unrelated authorized work.
- Do not infer a new power or increased discretion merely because a higher-order rule has changed.
- Preserve the historical validity of previously authorized acts unless a governing provision explicitly states otherwise.
- Escalate unresolved conflicts through the authorized review path.

This **authority reconciliation** is a required consequence of the change, not an optional cleanup task. The representation of dependencies and the update process will be specified in the resource-management protocols under outset item 2.

## 4. Delegation, review, and disputed cases

For each delegated function, the applicable instrument should establish its purpose, scope, permitted operations, constraints, review requirements, and authority for revision.

The following change classes are initially proposed:

| Class | Examples | Required treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine maintenance | Formatting, links, navigation, nonbinding summaries | Authorized maintainer; record change |
| Scientific record | Entering or correcting evidence, conclusions, or uncertainties | Authorized research maintainer; preserve provenance and review required by epistemic standards |
| Binding subordinate protocol | Changing research procedures, evidence criteria, permissions, or mandatory reviews | Independent review and approval under the delegation; no self-expansion of authority |
| Founding charter or reserved power | Changing the mandate, founding authority, or powers not delegated | Research Director's ratification |

Where the applicable delegation permits it, substantive subordinate protocol changes may be approved by two independently authorized reviewers, or by an authorized reviewer and the Research Director. **Two agents cannot create new authority by mutually approving it.**

An agent facing a genuinely ambiguous case first seeks an authorized reviewer or review-capable subagent. If none is available, it records a reasoned approval request for later acceptance or rejection by the appropriate authority, rather than treating silence as permission. The actual request location and lifecycle will be established in outset item 2.

Review must judge the effect of a change, not merely the file path or nominal document type.

## 5. Epistemic discipline

Every consequential scientific statement must be identifiable as one of the following *kinds of assertion*, without requiring that they be represented as separate files:

- **Premise or assumption:** adopted as a starting condition, not a verified external fact.
- **Hypothesis or interpretation:** possible explanation or prediction, open to challenge.
- **Observation or sourced report:** what was measured, seen, or reported, with its provenance.
- **Derived claim:** what follows from explicitly identified premises and reasoning.
- **Evaluated finding:** a conclusion whose support, scope, limitations, and objections have been assessed.
- **Decision:** an authorized choice, not itself evidence that a scientific claim is true.

A claim's confidence, applicability, and unresolved objections must not be lost merely because its status label changes. Absence of a known objection is not proof.

Research conclusions should retain, to the degree material to their use: claim; provenance; source quality; relevant conditions; method of inference or verification; support; counterevidence; scope; known limitations; uncertainty; and implications.

The precise resource schema and record format are reserved for outset item 2.

## 6. Evidentiary standards and validation

Research outputs must distinguish directly observed evidence, externally reported findings, deductions, and speculative interpretations.

Before adopting a substantive conclusion, verify at a level appropriate to its intended use:

1. **Source relevance and fidelity:** the cited source actually supports the proposition and was not distorted.
2. **Inference validity:** conclusions follow from the stated assumptions and evidence.
3. **Boundary conditions:** specify what cases, models, settings, and tasks were and were not investigated.
4. **Alternative explanations:** consider plausible competing interpretations and disconfirming evidence.
5. **Reproducibility or independent checks:** obtain them where the consequence and cost justify doing so.
6. **Uncertainty:** record remaining doubts without resolving them by confident wording.

A failed, inconclusive, or negative investigation is a valid output when its evidentiary implications are represented faithfully.

External sources discovered before the approval of this protocol must not be automatically promoted into the program's accepted knowledge; they will be independently assessed during the later knowledge-initialization stage.

## 7. Novelty and value assessment

Novelty assessment requires comparing **capabilities**, not simply architectures, labels, or shared components. A working comparison should explicitly identify what each existing method can do that the proposal cannot, and what the proposal aims to do that existing methods cannot. Distinguish documented functions from hands-on verification, inferred capacities, and unimplemented aspirations.

Two depths of audit are permitted:

- **Preliminary scan:** fast source and capability reconnaissance, useful for deciding what deserves deeper inquiry. Conclusions must remain provisional and must not prematurely narrow the research category.
- **Deeper capability audit:** inspect original publications, documentation, implementations, usability, costs, conditions of success, and contrary results as required for a defensible novelty claim.

Efficiency, reproduction, error detection, integration, and usability may be valuable contributions even where strict novelty is limited. The burden of evidence must be proportionate to the claim.

## 8. Research activity and limits

Research must occur under an explicit or reasonably derived mandate that identifies its purpose, expected type of outcome, constraints, and applicable approval boundary.

Exploration may reveal unknown unknowns and generate new candidate questions without pretending that they have been established. Investigation tests specific uncertainties. Development builds methods or instruments. Consolidation evaluates what those activities actually demonstrated.

A result should first be assessed for its epistemic scope; choosing what to investigate next is a distinct executive decision. Planning resource commitments is a third, separately governed function. Outset item 5 will specify the continuing procedures for those functions.

Before completion of applicable outset tasks and the problem-establishment assessment, substantial substantive research requires express authorization. Preparatory work to establish the program's standards is authorized under the charter.

## 9. Reporting, handoff, and correction

Research work products should make it possible to determine:

- What was authorized and undertaken.
- Which claims or questions were examined and by what means.
- What was actually established, narrowed, excluded, or left open.
- Where evidence, provenance, calculations, or implementation artifacts reside.
- What discrepancies or procedural failures occurred.
- What possible follow-on actions exist, without automatically authorizing them.

Incorrect claims must be corrected with a traceable record rather than silently rewritten into the appearance of continuous certainty. Contradictory results remain visible until reconciled or properly qualified.

## 10. Revision and adoption

This protocol is **a proposal**, not binding law, until adopted under the charter's governance rules. Its draft author may revise it, but adoption or material alteration of the program's research standards requires the Research Director's approval at the founding stage.

Once adopted, routine edits may be delegated. Binding amendments must follow the authority and independent-review requirements in section 4, as further codified through the program's governance instruments. An amendment affecting the source or scope of authority also triggers section 3.

**Outset item 1 is not complete merely because this file exists.** It is complete when the required standards are reviewed, adopted, and judged adequate by the Research Director. Outset items 2–5 remain separate and unauthorized for execution by this document alone.
