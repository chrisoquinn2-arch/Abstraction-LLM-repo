## Founding question
How do intelligent systems discover and revise appropriate responsibilities when identities, roles, objectives and specifications are abstract or incomplete? Understanding what the role requires is itself part of performing it.

## Opening concerns — not established defects
The Director described experiences suggesting premature interpretive commitment, weak planning and coordination, loss of context, inadequate epistemic qualification and poor integration of new nuance in sustained agent collaborations. These are observations and motives requiring careful evaluation; they may vary by model/context and may have existing solutions.

## Scientific research directions to preserve as questions
- What does successful open-ended role interpretation require, and how can it be measured?
- How can individual and cooperating agents discover overlooked obligations and reconsider earlier interpretations?
- Which architectures, training strategies, memory systems, scaffolds or coordination practices already solve portions of the problem, and which capabilities remain missing?
- How should autonomy, deliberation, governance and human/agent interaction be evaluated without defining success solely in terms of a favored method?
- Which empirical settings make the alleged problem meaningful, and what evidence would show the problem has been overestimated or already resolved?

## Research practice constraints from the founding program
1. **Capability-first novelty assessment:** compare what proposed and existing methods can and cannot do in both directions. A fast scan may generate hypotheses but cannot justify a strong novelty conclusion; deeper evidence-based audits are required for confident judgments.
2. **Epistemic scoping is distinct from executive prioritization, planning and execution.** Findings should track established, narrowed, excluded and open research space. Priorities use that information but should not rewrite the epistemic outcome.
3. **Scientific independence:** no favored architecture or research theory can exclude alternatives by default.
4. **Recursive, revisable inquiry:** new evidence can return the program to premises, questions, evaluation design, or strategy, with authority and source records preserved.
5. **Human and AI collaboration:** the infrastructure must permit people to intervene directly, challenge norms, contribute evidence, and inspect ongoing work without hidden inaccessible state.
