# Research Program Charter
## Intelligence and Abstract Identities

**Version:** 0.3 — Working founding draft  
**Status:** Proposed; not ratified  
**Founding authority:** Research Director  
**Effective date:** Upon ratification

---

### Article I — Establishment and Purpose

This charter proposes the establishment of a research program concerned with understanding and developing intelligent behavior under abstract identities, roles, objectives, and specifications.

The program is particularly concerned with situations in which discovering what a role requires is itself part of performing that role.

Its purpose is to investigate these capabilities, determine their significance and limitations, and pursue the knowledge and methods by which they may be understood, developed, and improved.

The program does not presume that its initial diagnoses or preferred solutions are correct.

### Article II — Background and Reason for Establishment

Intelligent activity often occurs under specifications that cannot fully enumerate the responsibilities, judgments, and procedures needed for their fulfillment. Professional, administrative, scientific, engineering, and organizational roles may be stated through broad objectives while their practical implications must be developed through inquiry, experience, and interaction.

An agent working under such a role may need not only to execute instructions but also to discover relevant questions, identify overlooked obligations, distinguish established from uncertain interpretations, recognize when previous interpretations need revision, and adapt its behavior to changing circumstances.

The founding concern is that present artificial intelligence systems may be unreliable at these activities, particularly during sustained, cooperative, or executive work. Experience with model-based workflows motivates investigation of apparent premature interpretive commitment, weak coordination, poor uncertainty handling, and difficulty integrating newly discovered nuances. These are **opening concerns, not established scientific findings**.

The relevant failures may have multiple causes, may occur only under particular conditions, or may already be addressed by existing approaches. Establishing whether a substantial unresolved problem exists is therefore a necessary early undertaking.

This uncertainty motivates a continuing research program rather than immediate commitment to a specific architecture or experiment. The program will examine whether and how intelligent systems can discover and refine appropriate behavior under abstract specifications and develop the necessary capabilities.

Its founding rationale is both investigative and developmental, with room to revise the initial framing as knowledge grows.

### Article III — Foundational Mandate

The program is authorized, subject to its approved governance and operating standards, to investigate:

1. Abstract identities, roles, and specifications, including their interpretation and development.
2. The actual capabilities, limitations, and failure modes of intelligent systems faced with incomplete or evolving specifications.
3. Discovery, refinement, reconsideration, and justified revision of responsibilities and operating behaviors.
4. Methods for acquiring, reinforcing, improving, and generalizing the relevant abilities.
5. Individual and cooperative intelligence, including executive, reflective, and metacognitive behavior.
6. Supporting theory, evaluations, experimental methods, instruments, and research infrastructure.

This is an initial domain of authority, not an exhaustive taxonomy or predefined project portfolio. Material extensions to the mandate require authorization under this charter.

### Article IV — Evidence-Guided Scientific Independence

The program shall remain independent of any favored theory, architecture, method, or technological implementation while allowing evidence to constrain its claims and research space.

Possibilities must not be excluded merely because they conflict with a current convention, preferred approach, or preliminary interpretation. Conversely, openness to a possibility does not make it credible, supported, or entitled to resources.

The program shall distinguish (a) what evidence establishes or excludes from (b) what investigations deserve priority. Hypotheses and methods may be narrowed, revised, or abandoned when warranted by evidence, relevance, feasibility, or justified decisions.

Reformulating an inadequate question, identifying a previously unknown distinction, or establishing that a suspected problem is already resolved can constitute meaningful progress.

### Article V — Mandate for Living Research Resources

The program shall establish and maintain an extensible set of governed resources that support continuous research. The initial resource responsibilities are:

- **Knowledge and theory:** relevant understanding, claims, their provenance, and unresolved interpretations.
- **Problems and research space:** suspected and established problems, unknowns, limits of results, and remaining possibilities.
- **Candidate solutions and methods:** approaches, their intended applications, prerequisites, alternatives, and supporting evidence.
- **Investigations and experiments:** proposed and conducted studies, observations, limitations, and their implications.
- **Protocols and standards:** applicable procedures, validation standards, and their revision histories.
- **Program governance and executive state:** authority, decisions, priorities, plans, dependencies, and commitments.
- **Research infrastructure:** instruments, tools, and facilities required to sustain or improve the program.

These are mandates for *resources and continuing responsibilities*, not a permanent directory layout or immutable classification scheme.

At the outset, the program shall define the meaning, representation, relationships, maintenance procedures, and initial contents of these resources under approved subordinate instruments.

Thereafter, authorized maintainers may revise their contents and, with appropriate governance, propose or enact additions, mergers, subdivisions, redefinitions, and retirement of categories. The program shall be able to recognize inadequacies in its own classifications.

### Article VI — Research Integrity and Value

The program is governed by these principles:

- **Epistemic accountability and traceability:** distinguish premises, hypotheses, evidence, findings, interpretations, and decisions; connect consequential conclusions to supporting grounds.
- **Scope discipline:** state what evidence establishes, what it does not, and what remains open.
- **Revisability:** permit warranted challenge, correction, and refinement of conclusions.
- **Evidence-guided independence:** preserve admissible possibilities without treating all as equally credible.
- **Evaluation integrity:** do not let a favored method determine its own standards of success without scrutiny.
- **Novelty and prior capability:** assess proposed contributions through evidence-grounded comparisons of what existing approaches can and cannot do. Preliminary scans may guide exploration but must not be represented as exhaustive audits.
- **Research efficiency:** attend to information value, alternatives, reuse of existing work, computational and human cost, and avoidable duplication.
- **Proportionality:** scale review, evidence, and authorization to the consequences, uncertainty, cost, and reversibility of the work.
- **Discovery openness:** allow results to reveal new questions, distinctions, or errors in the research framing.

Scientific novelty is one possible form of value, not a prerequisite for useful replication, verification, infrastructure, or negative results.

Detailed implementations of these principles belong to subordinate standards and protocols.

### Article VII — Governance, Derived Authority, and Delegation

The Research Director is the founding authority of the program and retains responsibility for ratification of the charter and any powers not lawfully delegated.

Authority may arise by explicit delegation or by a reasonable, accountable interpretation of already codified authority. Delegations and subordinate governance instruments must remain traceable to an authorized source. Agents need not seek fresh permission for ordinary actions within their authority.

Authorized agents may maintain and revise resources, develop methods, and manage delegated activities. The authority to edit text is distinct from authority to alter its binding meaning or enlarge delegated powers. The effect of a change, not merely the file containing it, determines the applicable approval requirement.

An agent facing an unresolved authority question shall seek review through available authorized reviewers or subagents. Where no such review path is available, it shall record a reasoned approval request in designated governance infrastructure for decision. Interpretation of existing authority does not authorize agents to invent unrestricted powers.

Subordinate governance instruments shall define roles, review procedures, escalation, and means for recording decisions without contradicting this charter.

### Article VIII — Temporary Outset Mandate

Upon ratification, the program is authorized and directed to undertake initial establishment work, including:

1. Establishing the program's research protocols, evidence standards, and governance procedures.
2. Defining and initializing the resources mandated by Article V, including their meaning, relationships, maintenance, and authorized revisions.
3. Recording opening beliefs, preliminary knowledge, and candidate questions with appropriate epistemic status, without presuming their validity.
4. Establishing the problem-worthiness assessment and determining whether an adequately evidenced research problem or opportunity justifies the program's substantive investigations.
5. Establishing procedures for research-scoping, executive prioritization, planning, authorization, review, and continuation.

The Research Director shall determine when this initial mandate has been satisfactorily completed. The *temporary establishment mandate* then expires; the enduring standards and resources created under it remain operative until lawfully revised.

Establishment work may include limited, expressly authorized fact-finding necessary for its gates. It does not itself authorize unrestricted substantive research.

### Article IX — Research Development and Continuity

The program may maintain conventional charters for subordinate projects, research plans, schedules, milestones, budgets, and contingencies alongside evolving scientific understanding.

The program shall distinguish epistemic scoping (what is known, excluded, or still possible), executive prioritization (which unknowns are most productive to pursue), program planning (what resources and work to commit), and research execution (conducting and evaluating investigations).

These functions interact but shall not be treated as equivalent. New discoveries can justify reviewing any of them without implying that conventional planning is incompatible with uncertain research.

### Article X — Amendments, Review, and Ratification

This charter may be amended only through the authority and procedure established for constitutional changes, initially requiring the Research Director's ratification.

Subordinate resources and protocols may be amended under delegated authority. The program is authorized to establish review tiers in which ordinary changes are made by authorized maintainers and consequential protocol amendments require independent review—for example two appropriately authorized agents or an agent and the Research Director.

No approval quorum may enlarge its own authority beyond powers derived from the charter or a valid delegation. Changes affecting foundational authority require the applicable higher authorization.

Exceptional, ambiguous, or contested cases shall be referred to an authorized reviewer or subagent; where unavailable, a documented approval request shall be deposited in designated infrastructure. Approval, rejection, and the grounds for the decision shall be recorded under the applicable protocol.

All binding amendments shall be traceable and versioned. The detailed amendment classes, quorum rules, conflict resolution process, and review infrastructure shall be defined in subordinate governance instruments established under Article VIII.
